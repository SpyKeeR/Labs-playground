Background / Scenario

As the number of IIoT devices continue to grow at an exponential rate, so are the number of attacks on these devices. There are a wide variety of attacks on IIoT devices that have been documented and reported on the news.

As an IT professional working with IIoT devices, it is important to be aware of the wide range of attacks and vulnerabilities that have occurred on these devices. It is also important to know how to defend against and minimize the damage from these types of attacks.

How was PipeDream / Incontroller malware attack mitigated?

  1. Early Detection and Reverse Engineering
    • Security researchers from Dragos, SentinelOne, and IBM X-Force quickly analyzed the malware after its appearance in January 2020.
    • They reverse-engineered the EKANS binary and identified:
      • A hardcoded list of over 60 process names, including ICS-related ones (e.g., HMI, SCADA, PLC tools)
      • File encryption behavior and system shutdown routines
  1. Deep Technical Analysis
    • Analysts reverse-engineered the malware and broke it into five core components:
      • Tools for manipulating PLCs (especially from Omron and Schneider Electric)
      • Scanners for ICS protocols (Modbus, OPC UA, IEC-104, CODESYS)
      • Components for device discovery and lateral movement
    • Dragos and Mandiant published detailed reports outlining the malware’s functionality and indicators of compromise (IOCs).
  1. Coordinated Disclosure to Affected Vendors
    • ICS vendors (e.g., Schneider Electric, Omron) were notified early.
    • Vendors issued advisories, firmware updates, and guidance to customers for hardening systems.
  1. Defensive Recommendations Issued
    • Organizations received action plans, including:
      • Disabling unused ICS protocols and services
      • Segmenting OT and IT networks
      • Implementing strict access control policies for engineering workstations and PLCs
      • Monitoring for specific PipeDream indicators, including suspicious commands or unexpected protocol use
  1. Government and Industry Coordination
    • The U.S. CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) released a joint advisory (April 2022) with Dragos, Mandiant, and other federal partners.
    • Shared IOCs and tactics with critical infrastructure providers and sector-specific Information Sharing and Analysis Centers (ISACs).

How was EKANS (a.k.a. Snake) Ransomware malware attack mitigated?

  1. Early Detection and Reverse Engineering
    • Security researchers from Dragos, SentinelOne, and IBM X-Force quickly analyzed the malware after its appearance in January 2020.
    • They reverse-engineered the EKANS binary and identified:
      • A hardcoded list of over 60 process names, including ICS-related ones (e.g., HMI, SCADA, PLC tools)
      • File encryption behavior and system shutdown routines
    This rapid analysis allowed Incident Response (IR) teams to identify Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) and create YARA rules and antivirus definitions.
  1. OT-IT Network Segmentation
    • Organizations with segmented IT and OT networks were less impacted, as the ransomware primarily entered through IT systems.
    • Critical infrastructure operators with firewalls, demilitarized zones (DMZs), and strict remote access controls limited EKANS from reaching OT assets.
  1. Access Control and VPN Hardening
    • EKANS often exploited weak Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) settings or poorly configured VPNs to enter systems.
    • Mitigation included:
      • Disabling unnecessary RDP access
      • Enforcing multi-factor authentication (MFA)
      • Patching known vulnerabilities in Citrix, Pulse Secure, and Fortinet VPNs
  1. Endpoint Protection and Backup Restoration
    • Companies with advanced endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools caught the malware early.
    • Organizations with offline or immutable backups were able to restore systems without paying ransom.
    • Backup validation and isolation became a key lesson learned for others.
  1. Threat Intelligence Sharing
    • CISA and major cybersecurity vendors issued public alerts and technical reports (e.g., CISA Alert AA20-049A).
    • Shared:
      • IOC hashes
      • Affected process names
      • Recommended firewall rules and network monitoring signatures
  1. Process Whitelisting and Application Control
    • EKANS relied on killing specific ICS processes before encryption.
    • Defenders began:
      • Whitelisting allowed applications
      • Blocking unauthorized process termination attempts
      • Implementing application control on ICS workstations

A sample answer below:

How was the Industroyer2 malware attack mitigated?

  1. Rapid Detection and Intelligence Sharing
    • CERT-UA and ESET detected Industroyer2 before it executed its full payload.
    • Ukrainian defenders were actively monitoring systems due to heightened tensions and prior experience from 2015–2016.
    • The malware was set to activate on a specific date (April 8, 2022), but was discovered several days earlier, allowing intervention.
  1. Reverse Engineering of the Malware
    • Malware analysts quickly dissected Industroyer2:
      • It was modular and tailored to specific substations.
      • Designed to send malicious IEC-104 commands to circuit breakers and disconnect substations.
    • The early discovery enabled defenders to:
      • Isolate the malware
      • Create detection rules (IOCs and YARA signatures)
      • Alert others in Ukraine and internationally
  1. Manual Operator Intervention
    • Ukrainian grid operators were prepared to respond to cyber sabotage.
    • They reportedly used manual fallback procedures to:
      • Keep the grid stable
      • Prevent physical damage to substations
    • The malware had limited ability to adapt on the fly, making manual intervention effective.
  1. System Restoration and Recovery
    • Some systems were temporarily disrupted, but no long-term damage occurred.
    • CERT-UA coordinated with operators to:
      • Remove the malware
      • Restore supervisory control
      • Implement emergency failover procedures
  1. International Support and Coordination
    • Ukraine received support from NATO partners and private cybersecurity firms.
    • Tools, expertise, and real-time intelligence helped prevent the attack from escalating.
    • This case demonstrated the value of cyber resilience partnerships during wartime.
